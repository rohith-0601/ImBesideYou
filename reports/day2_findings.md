## 1. Day 1's process taxonomy was wrong, and the error mattered

Day 1 labelled processes from the SPA route alone — `#/payroll-items` →
`payroll_change`. That was incorrect.

**Dataset B contains three separate business systems, and they reuse the same
route names.** `browser_navigation` payloads carry both `url` and
`page_title`, which is how the mapping falls out:

| port | `page_title` | system |
|---|---|---|
| `127.0.0.1:5132` | HR人事給与システム | HR / payroll |
| `127.0.0.1:5133` | 財務会計システム | financial accounting |
| `127.0.0.1:5134` | 受発注在庫管理システム | order / inventory management |

The same route is unrelated work on different systems. `#/social-insurance`
is welfare applications on HR, **budget variance analysis** (予算差異分析) on
Finance, and **IT equipment requests** (IT申請) on Inventory.
`#/leave-applications` is genuine leave on HR, **managerial expense approval**
on Finance, and **contract management** (契約管理) on Inventory.

So process identity is the **(system, route)** pair. Route-only labelling had
merged invoice approval, HR payroll changes and inventory work into one class.

**This is also the real explanation for Day 1's `payroll_change` anomaly.**
Day 1 saw 96 "cases" at a 6.7s median and guessed list-view rendering was
inflating the count. It wasn't. `#/payroll-items` existed on all three
systems, so three unrelated processes were being counted as one. The guessed
cause and the actual cause were different, which is a good argument for not
acting on the Day 1 hypothesis before testing it.

Corrected: **12 resolvable processes**, each verified against the vocabulary
on its own screen.

## 2. What the `P<n>-<digits>-<digits>` strings actually are

Day 1 treated these as case IDs and counted 170 of them. They are UIA row
names (`control_type: "DataItem"`), and they decompose as
**`P<process>-<batch>-<row>`**:

- the 8-digit body is **constant within a session** (16 distinct values over
  15 sessions), so it identifies the run, not the case;
- the `P<n>` prefix maps essentially **1:1 onto (system, route)** — it is a
  *process code*;
- the 3-digit tail is the **row number** within the screen's table.

A screenshot settles it: the Finance invoice table's `ID` column literally
lists `P6-07010448-001` … `-012`, one per row, each paired in the 区分 column
with an invoice number `請求書承認 INV-2026-7344` … `-7355`. So `P6-…-001`
and `INV-2026-7344` are two identifiers for the same case.

This turned a Day 1 mistake into the strongest asset available: because
`p_code` is produced by the portal independently of the URL, it is a
**cross-check on the labels** rather than a label source. 11 of 12 P-codes
sit in exactly one (system, route) at 82–100%.

## 3. The segmentation model

An **execution** is a contiguous episode of work within one process context.
This mirrors the GT schema in DATA_SCHEMA.md, which records
`process_started` / `process_switched_out` / `process_suspended` /
`process_resumed` — ground truth tracks process *episodes* and treats
interruption as suspend/resume, so a worker who leaves and returns produces
two episodes with the same label. That is exactly what the deliverable asks
for ("use the same label for the same process").

Two signals were rejected, both on measurement rather than taste:

- **Idle gaps** (Day 1): 99.9th percentile gap is 10.1s; only 7 of 20,477
  events exceed 30s, because the README says waiting time was compressed in
  the test recording.
- **Case IDs as the unit**: the only genuine per-item IDs are the 72
  `INV-2026-nnnn` invoice references, covering 1 process of 12. Case IDs are
  therefore kept for sub-counting inside `fin_invoice_matching` on Day 3, not
  used as the segmentation unit.

**Result: 165 episodes → 601 case executions across 15 sessions, zero
overlap, 95.4% mean coverage of session wall-clock** (93.4–97.4%). Day 1's
naive case spans summed to 1.45×–3.32× wall-clock; that failure mode is
measurably gone. See §8 for why executions, not episodes, are the
deliverable.

## 4. Signal availability forced a two-source design

`browser_url` is populated on only **37–66%** of events, and on **0%** of
`ses_20260701-192455-NEELA9BAF`, which recorded **no L3 events at all** — the
browser extension never connected. So URL alone cannot carry the pipeline.

`window_title` names the system on **96.6–100%** of Edge events in every
session including that one, and agrees with the URL port **96.5%** of the
time (n=9,362). It gives the system but never the route.

Design consequence: the (port, route) pair is the primary label source and is
forward-filled **atomically**; `window_title` validates it.

For the L3-less session, the route is recovered from the **process code the
portal stamps into clicked row names** — `p_code` needs no browser extension,
and the worker stays on a screen between clicks, so it forward-fills
cleanly. That resolved the session completely: all 12 processes, no
`*_unresolved` labels anywhere in the deliverable. `P8` is deliberately
excluded from the recovery map as the one ambiguous code, so a stretch
resolvable only by `P8` would still fall back to system granularity rather
than being guessed.

## 5. Two bugs worth recording

**(a) Independent forward-fill manufactured three phantom processes.** Filling
`port` and `route` as separate columns let a stale route from one system
attach to a newly-focused other system. That produced `hr_resident_tax`,
`inv_order_check` and `inv_inventory_review` — each a handful of events, each
with **no `p_code` support and no Japanese business vocabulary**, which is how
they were caught. Filling the pair atomically removed all three. Worth noting
that the phantom labels looked perfectly plausible in the output table; only
the corroborating-signal check exposed them.

**(b) `float('nan')` is truthy.** The guard
`if p and r and r not in IGNORED_ROUTES` passed for NaN ports, building a
literal `"nan|nan"` pair for 11,042 events and collapsing label coverage to
45.2%. Fixed with `isinstance(x, str)`. Coverage went to 100%.

## 6. The screenshot spot-check caught a flaw — in the checker

The first spot-check appeared to show a false boundary: both frames either
side of a `fin_invoice_matching` → `hr_expense_and_payroll_change` boundary
showed the *same* Finance invoice screen.

Reading the raw events there showed **the segmentation was right** — there is
a real `browser_navigation` to `127.0.0.1:5132/#/payroll-items` at that
instant — and the tool was wrong, for two measurable reasons:

- a capture 0.1s after a navigation still shows the **old** page (the
  pre-navigation frame even has the browser loading bar visible);
- `window_title` **trails the navigation by ~2.3s**, so titles also still
  name the old system briefly.

Fixed by taking captures at a standoff (≥2s before, ≥4s after). A re-checked
boundary then verified cleanly: before is Notepad `IT申請メモ`
("PC新規発行申請 / 申請者：坂本 裕二"), after is 受発注在庫管理システム with
**契約管理** highlighted in its sidebar, showing a 保守委託契約（更新）
approval form. The label `inv_contract_management` matches the screen's own
menu name.

The lesson is about evidence, not code: an internal metric said 99.3% of
boundaries aligned with navigation events, and that metric is nearly
tautological — segments are cut *because* of navigation. Only looking at
pixels tested anything the algorithm didn't already assume.

## 7. Carried into Day 3

- **`fin_invoice_matching` is the largest process by time** (16 segments,
  1,238s, 67.4s median) and is the one with genuine per-case IDs, so its
  volume can be counted exactly rather than estimated.
- **Word is concentrated, not spread.** `inv_contract_management` is 1,087
  Word events and `fin_payment_processing` 648 — and the contract screen
  shows `参照：参照書類：shinkui_keiyaku_tetsuzuki.docx`, i.e. the portal
  *names a document the worker must open*. That is a much more specific and
  more automatable finding than Day 1's "Word is the second-heaviest app".
- **`fin_budget_variance_analysis` is 497 Excel events of 714** — nearly
  pure spreadsheet work, a different automation shape from the portal flows.
- Per-operator and per-variant analysis (the README's "different handling
  patterns" question) is now possible, since segments carry label, duration
  and operator.

**Known limitation to state in the report:** boundary accuracy is still
unmeasured against truth. What is established is that the segments are
non-overlapping, cover 95.6% of wall-clock, agree 98.6% with an independent
process code, use one label per process, and survive visual inspection at
sampled boundaries. That is materially weaker than an F1 against `gt.jsonl`
and should not be presented as equivalent.

## 8. Optimisation: episodes are not executions

The first Day 2 pipeline emitted 162 **episodes** — contiguous stretches of
work in one process context. That under-segments, and the data says so
plainly: those episodes contain **588 case-completion markers**, a median of
3–4 each. An invoice clerk works `INV-2026-7345`, then `-7347`, then `-7348`,
never leaving the screen. One episode, three executions.

The README asks to segment into "individual executions of business
processes", and dataset A's `gt_manifest.json` fixes the granularity:
`executions[]` is "one entry per execution of that process", each carrying its
own `case_id`. So episodes were roughly 3.6× too coarse.

**The marker.** Every process closes each case by writing a completion comment
that names it:

```
請求書照合完了。INV-2026-7347　金額：1,832,962円。差異なし承認。
給与変更登録。変更種別：残業手当調整。適用日：2026-07-02。確認完了。
発注管理処理。スポット発注：梱包材料　数量 164　合計 6,314,164円　通常。発注書確認・登録完了。
在庫調整登録。品番：BATCH-W2　品名：電源モジュールB　調整数：+155個。
```

Detection is **structural, not keyword-based**, and that choice was forced by
a failure: an early version keyed on 完了|承認|済み and found only 8 markers
for `inv_stock_adjustment` instead of 82, because stock adjustments are logged
as memos with no completion verb. The rule that works is "a field value long
enough to be a comment, that is not the field's placeholder" — placeholders
are identifiable because they all end in an ellipsis
(`処理内容・確認コメントを入力してください…`).

**Why deduplicating by text is safe.** `target_field.value` on a keystroke
carries the field's *current* value, so progressive typing would emit partial
prefixes and inflate the count. Checked before relying on it: across a full
session's 86 long values, **zero** are a strict prefix of a nearby later
value. The comments are pasted, not typed character-by-character — which is
also why `clipboard_change` events accompany them.

The split conserves the episode's span exactly, so coverage and
non-overlap are unchanged.

**Result: 601 executions, 12 labels, zero overlap, 95.4% coverage, 97.8%
`p_code` agreement** (583/596).

### The evaluation got harder, which is the point

Execution-boundary alignment with navigation events fell from 96.0% (episode
level) to **75.1%**, and that drop is the desired outcome. Most case
boundaries occur *inside* one screen, where there is no navigation to align
with — so a high score there would have meant the executions were not being
split at all. `evaluate.py` now reports both granularities and says which
number means what, because quoting only the flattering one would misrepresent
what was tested.

## 9. Label correction: 発注管理, not bank reconciliation

`5133|#/resident-tax` was labelled `fin_bank_reconciliation` on the strength
of the vendor names on screen (関西物流サービス, 北陸部品工業株式会社). The
completion comments overturn that:

```
発注管理処理。スポット発注：梱包材料　数量 164　合計 6,314,164円　通常。発注書確認・登録完了。
発注管理処理。年間契約：サーバー保守部品　数量 425　合計 19,877,675円　通常。発注書確認・登録完了。
```

This is **発注管理 (purchase order management)** — spot orders and annual
contracts, with quantities and totals. It corroborates independently: the
Finance sidebar in the screenshots lists 請求書承認・経費精算, 経費承認（管理職）,
支払処理, 予算差異分析 and 発注管理, and 発注管理 was the only item with no
route assigned. Renamed `fin_purchase_order_management`.

Worth noting how the error happened: vendor names are consistent with both
readings, and I picked the wrong one from a plausible-looking partial signal.
The completion comments are the process naming *itself*, which makes them
better evidence than the row contents. Only reachable after building the
execution splitter — the optimisation paid for itself twice.
