## 1. Step 2 answered

**601 executions, 14 process labels, 4 operators, 15 sessions.** Every
execution carries the completion comment its worker wrote, which names the
process, the handling pattern, and often the amount and the decision. So the
README's Step 2 questions are read off the data rather than inferred.

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| What processes are performed? | 12 distinct back-office processes across three systems (HR payroll, financial accounting, order/inventory) |
| How often? | 15–90 executions each over ~3 hours of recording |
| How much time? | 294–1,288s each; total 10,076s of attributed process time |
| How many people? | 4 operators; every process is performed by 2–4 of them, none is a single-person silo |
| Different handling patterns? | Yes — 2–11 named variants per process, stated explicitly in the comment |

## 2. One screen hosts two processes

`5132|#/payroll-items` was labelled `hr_expense_and_payroll_change` on Day 2.
The completion comments show it is actually **two** processes sharing a
screen:

- `給与変更登録。変更種別：昇給。適用日：2026-07-06。確認完了。` — a payroll
  master change (**19 executions**, 5 variants: 昇給, 役職手当新設,
  役職手当廃止, 扶養控除変更, 残業手当調整)
- `経費精算確認済み。費目：出張旅費　金額：42,838円。規程内であることを確認した。`
  — an employee expense settlement check (**90 executions**, 5 variants:
  交通費精算, 出張旅費, 消耗品費, 研修費, 接待交際費)

Different inputs, different approvers, very different automation profiles.
Counting them as one 111-execution process with "ten variants" would have
overstated both the volume and the branching of a candidate that then ranks
near the top. Split into `hr_payroll_change` and `hr_expense_settlement`.

This is the third time on this task that a screen-derived label turned out
coarser than the real process, after the route/system conflation on Day 2.
The pattern is consistent: **the portal's navigation structure is not the
business process structure.**

## 3. The completion comment outranks the screen context

Day 2 labelled each execution from the forward-filled (system, route) pair.
Checked against the comment template, excluding the HR screen from §2 (whose
relabelling is the split, not a correction):

- where a URL was live: **415/419 agree (99.0%)**
- where the comment was written in Notepad or Excel (no URL): **41/62 (66.1%)**

The inherited context is simply wrong for comments typed outside the browser
— it carries whichever portal screen happened to precede. Since the comment
states its own process, it wins.

**133 of 601 executions are relabelled in total**, and they are two different
things worth separating:

| | n | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| HR screen split | 108 | `hr_expense_and_payroll_change` resolved into `hr_payroll_change` / `hr_expense_settlement` — a refinement, not an error |
| Genuine context mislabels | 25 | almost all comments typed in Notepad or Excel, where there was no URL to inherit from |

`segments.jsonl` regenerated accordingly.

Two executions have no completion comment at all and keep the Day 2
screen-level label. They are left visible rather than force-assigned.

**`fin_invoice_matching` is the largest process by time and ranks 7th.** Two
measured reasons: 34.4% of its cases end in 差異あり要確認 ("discrepancy,
needs checking") rather than 差異なし承認, so a third of the work is human
judgement that cannot be automated away; and only 25% of its executions stay
in the browser — the rest pull in Excel and Word. Ranking by size alone would
have picked it, and it is the wrong first target.

`inv_contract_management` is second-largest by time and ranks 8th for a
related reason: 17% browser-only, because the portal displays
`参照：参照書類：shinkui_keiyaku_tetsuzuki.docx` and the worker must open that
document. The Day 2 lead was right that this is highly automatable *in
principle* — but "open the named Word document" is a desktop integration, not
an HTTP call.

### The ranking is stable

Under five different weightings, `hr_leave_application` ranks 1st in four and
7th in the fifth — the exception being "ignore feasibility entirely", which
is precisely the weighting that produces the wrong answer. `hr_expense_settlement`
ranks 1–3 in every scenario. The top three are not an artefact of the formula;
see §5 of [`day3_analysis.md`](day3_analysis.md).

## 5. The most important feasibility finding: the terminal action is invisible

Across all 20,477 events there are **7 `✓ 承認` clicks and 4 `⏸ 保留` clicks**.
Only 1.6% of `fin_invoice_matching` executions contain an approve-style click
at all.

We can see the worker select a record, consult a document, and write a
completion comment. **We essentially never see the submit.** L3 browser
coverage is 2,764 of 20,477 events (13.5%), and UIA rarely names the button.

This is the single biggest risk in the whole proposal, and it is evidence-
based rather than hypothetical: *we would be automating a flow whose final
step we have never actually observed.* We do not know the endpoint, the
payload, whether it is idempotent, or what it returns on failure. Any plan
that assumes "and then POST the approval" is assuming the part the data does
not cover.

Mitigation is not analytical — it requires one hour with the real system, or
a single instrumented recording where the extension stays connected. That is
Day 4's first task, deliberately, because if the submit path cannot be driven
the whole shape of Step 3 changes.

## 6. Other risks, each tied to its evidence

| risk | evidence |
|---|---|
| **Browser instrumentation is unreliable** — anything depending on DOM events is fragile | L3 is 13.5% of events; one session of 15 recorded **zero** L3 events because the extension never connected |
| **We do not know the input schema** — only that fields exist | `clipboard_change.text_content` and `browser_form_input.value` are null throughout; `capture_full_keystrokes: false`, `redact_password_fields: true` |
| **Thin evidence per variant** | `hr_welfare_application` has 16 executions across 5 variants — 2–4 examples each. Rules inferred from that will be wrong |
| **Production auth is unknown** | all three systems are `127.0.0.1:513x` with no visible login; the real deployment will have SSO, roles and audit that the logs say nothing about |
| **Durations are compressed** | the README says so explicitly; used for *relative* ranking only, and no annualised saving is extrapolated |
| **Approval is a governance boundary** | these are HR/payroll and finance records — a wrong silent write is expensive and, for payroll, individually harmful |

## 7. Step 3 decision

**Build a shared review-and-approve foundation with per-process definitions,
and configure it for the top three processes.**

### Why a shared foundation rather than one bespoke tool

All twelve processes have the same shape, and this is measured, not assumed:
select a record → check it against a rule → write a **templated** completion
comment → submit. The templates are literally parameterised strings with the
variant in a slot:

```
勤怠申請確認。種別：{variant}。取得日：{date}。問題なし承認。
経費精算確認済み。費目：{category}　金額：{amount}円。規程内であることを確認した。
発注管理処理。{order_type}：{item}　数量 {qty}　合計 {amount}円　{urgency}。発注書確認・登録完了。
```

So a "process definition" is a small object — comment template, variant list,
validation rule, target screen. The marginal cost of the fourth process is a
config entry, not a code change. Building one bespoke tool for
`hr_leave_application` would deliver 690s of coverage; the same effort plus
three config entries covers 2,527s.

### Why these three

`hr_leave_application` (highest opportunity, 96.4% browser-only, zero
exceptions, 5 clean variants), `hr_expense_settlement` (highest volume at 90
executions, and its rule — 規程内であることを確認した, "confirmed within
policy" — is a threshold check against an amount, which is the most
mechanisable rule in the whole dataset), and `fin_purchase_order_management`
(68 executions, and its 13.2% 緊急 exception rate makes it the test case for
whether the exception path degrades gracefully).

Together: **214 of 601 executions (35.6%)** and **2,527s of 10,076s (25.1%)**.

### Why a web application, confirmed

The Day 1 reasoning holds and is now better supported. The portals are
already local web apps; a browser-based tool sits alongside them with no
desktop install, and gives the operator a review surface. Human-in-the-loop
is not a nicety here — with the submit path unverified (§5) and payroll
records at stake (§6), a tool that prepares and a human who commits is the
only defensible first version.

### What is explicitly deferred, and why

- **`fin_invoice_matching`** — largest by time, but 34% of cases need
  judgement and 75% touch Excel/Word. A later phase, once the deterministic
  path has produced labelled decisions that could train the discrepancy call.
- **`inv_contract_management`** — needs document handling (open the named
  `.docx`), a different and larger integration than the portal flows.
- **`fin_budget_variance_analysis`** — 497 of its 714 events are Excel. It is
  a spreadsheet automation, not a portal one; different tool entirely.
- **`inv_stock_adjustment`** — 11 variants on 81 executions is the thinnest
  evidence-per-branch in the set, despite decent volume.
- **Straight-through processing** — deferred on purpose, not on schedule.
  Nothing in the logs establishes that the submit is safe to automate.

## 8. Carried into Day 4

1. **Verify the submit path first.** If approval cannot be driven
   programmatically, the tool becomes a preparation-and-checklist surface
   rather than an execute surface — and that must be known before building,
   not after.
2. Lock the stack, scaffold, and get the app reading real portal data.
3. Define the per-process definition schema so the three configs are data,
   not code.
